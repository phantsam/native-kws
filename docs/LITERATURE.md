## 1. Benchmarks and protocols

**Dataset.** Google Speech Commands, v0.01 (64,721 clips, 30 words) and v0.02 (105,829 clips, 35 words), released with speaker-disjoint `validation_list.txt` / `testing_list.txt` generated by a filename-hash rule, plus separately released 12-class test sets (v1: 3,081 clips; v2: 4,890 clips) [V, Warden 2018; split counts reproduced in our pipeline].

**Three task definitions in common use:**
* **V1-12 / V2-12** — 10 keywords + `_unknown_` + `_silence_`.
* **V2-35** — all 35 words, no silence/unknown class.
* **V1-30** — all 30 words of v1 (reported by some papers, not used here).

**The protocol difference nobody states explicitly.** We read the official code of three baselines [V]:

| Paper | 12-class test set | Reported weights | Runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| BC-ResNet (`Qualcomm-AI-research/bcresnet`) | separately released official test set | final epoch | 1 |
| KWT (`ARM-software/keyword-transformer`, via `kws_streaming`) | built from `testing_list` + 10 % silence + 10 % unknown, seeded | final | mean ± std |
| Keyword Mamba (`dhyzy123/KWM`, NeMo-style script) | built from `testing_list` + 10 % unknown + 10 % silence (noise slices × U(0,1)) | final epoch (its `best_model` is a reference to the live model, not a copy) | mean of 3 |

**How much it matters, measured** (`kws/eval_protocols.py`): on V2 the two constructions share **byte-identical keyword test audio** (all 4,074 clips) and differ only in which 408 unknown clips are drawn and how silence is synthesised; the accuracy difference is ≤ 0.1 points. On **V1 the gap is larger (~0.2 points)**, which is enough to flip a comparison, and is why our V1-12 result is reported as ambiguous. V2-35 uses the plain `testing_list` everywhere and is directly comparable.

**Consequences for reporting.** Published numbers are *final-epoch, single-protocol* numbers. We therefore report final-epoch **and** best-by-validation accuracy, on **both** 12-class protocols, with 3 seeds (see `EXPERIMENTS.md` E6, E21, E22).

## 3. Architecture families

**Convolutional, MCU-oriented.** *Hello Edge* [V] established the DS-CNN baseline and the int8-on-Cortex-M framing, and remains the MLPerf Tiny KWS reference model. TC-ResNet [M] moved the temporal axis into channels for phone-class latency. MatchboxNet [M] used depthwise-separable 1-D convolutions at 93 K params. **BC-ResNet** [V] is the accuracy-per-parameter reference: most computation happens on a frequency-pooled 1-D signal and is *broadcast* back to 2-D, with SubSpectral Normalization (separate statistics per frequency sub-band, ICASSP 2021 [M]) stabilising the 2-D path. Residual KWS (Res15) [S] predates it.

**Attention.** KWT [V] applies a ViT-style transformer to the mel spectrogram, reaching 98.56 on V2-12 at 5.4 M params; accuracy scales with size rather than efficiency. Attention-based end-to-end KWS [S] is earlier work in the same direction.

**Attention-free.** Keyword-MLP [V] (PML4DC @ ICLR 2022) shows gated MLPs reach 97.56 on V2-35 at 424 K, i.e. self-attention is not necessary. Sparse binarization + a linear classifier (Interspeech 2024) [V] claims 4× faster than the prior best edge-compatible model.

**State-space.** **Keyword Mamba** [V] is, to its own knowledge, the first SSM for KWS: bidirectional Mamba layers replacing transformer layers (KWM), or replacing only the attention sub-layer while keeping the feed-forward (KWM-T); 12 layers, 40 MFCC, class token inserted mid-sequence, trained from scratch, 3-run averages. Its efficiency argument is per-parameter, not per-device: **it is not deployed on hardware, and its operator set has no MCU path** (see §7).

**Few-shot / open-vocabulary.** EdgeSpot (ICASSP 2026) [V] combines a BC-ResNet backbone with per-channel energy normalisation, light temporal self-attention and distillation from a self-supervised teacher: 10-shot accuracy 73.7 → 82.0 at 128 K params. Open-vocabulary KWS with hyper-matched filters for small-footprint devices (2025) [S]. Few-shot KWS in any language, prototypical-network KWS [S].

## 4. Training-side techniques (distillation, robustness)

* **Knowledge distillation** itself: Hinton et al. 2015 [M].
* **Ensemble KD for noise-robust small-footprint KWS**: a joint framework of noise-mixture curriculum learning and **weighted ensemble distillation** from snapshots, explicitly aimed at MCU-sized students, on Speech Commands with external noise sets (IEEE Access, 2023) [S]. **This is the closest prior work to our distillation setup** and must be cited; it means "ensemble KD into a tiny KWS model" is *not* novel.
* **Prototypical KD for noise-robust KWS** (2022) [S].
* **Low-bit quantization + QAT for KWS** (Amazon, ICMLA 2019) [V]: dynamic column-wise quantization, up to 80 % memory reduction at near-float accuracy.
* **Clean-teacher / noisy-student** training with parallel data is established in **ASR** (teacher-student learning for noise-robust ASR, 2019) [S]; applying it to KWS distillation is a small delta, not a contribution.
* **On-device personalisation**: on-device learnable user speech characteristics, 23.7 K params, error 30.1 → 24.3 % under speaker shift (tinyML Research Symposium 2024) [V]; on-device domain learning for KWS on extreme-edge systems (AICAS 2024) [S]; domain-incremental continual learning for KWS (arXiv, Jan 2026, unreviewed) [V].

**Relevant finding of ours.** Distillation consistently **traded low-SNR robustness for clean accuracy** in our runs (every distilled student was worse than its non-distilled counterpart at −5 dB SNR). We found no paper reporting this trade-off explicitly; the curriculum-learning work above is motivated by the opposite goal (making a *noise-robust* student), which suggests the interaction is known in practice but not isolated.

## 5. Efficiency: skipping, early exit, adaptive compute

* **Skip RNN** (ICLR 2018) [V]: the network *learns* to skip state updates, copying the hidden state instead of updating it; motivated by fewer sequential operations, lower energy, and longer effective memory. Follow-up: skipping RNN state updates **without retraining** (ML-on-edge workshop, 2019) [S]. Skip RNN has been used in KWS contexts [S]. **This makes "learn to skip frames" prior art**; any contribution of ours must be the *supervision signal*, the *exactness of skipping in a selective SSM*, and *measured device energy*.
* **Early-decision spiking KWS** (ED-sKWS) [S] decides as early as possible to save energy; progressive time-scaled curriculum distillation cuts SNN timesteps by 60 % and energy by 54.8 % [S].
* **Streaming KWS**: Google's `kws_streaming` library [V] converts non-streaming Keras KWS models to streaming mode with per-layer state, and MLPerf Tiny v1.3 added a **streaming wake-word** benchmark (1-D depthwise-separable CNN on continuous audio) [V] — so streaming evaluation is becoming standard, and a non-streaming claim is increasingly weak.
* **System-level efficiency**: an STM32 study (Sept 2025) [V] measures the **whole** pipeline (MFCC + inference) on N6/H7/U5 and reports 92.4 % F1 at 14.4 k params, finding that the NPU-equipped part wins on energy-delay product. A KWS implementation on NXP MCXN947 with an integrated NPU [V] reports 97.06 % with a 30.58 KB model and 59× speed-up over CPU execution.

## 6. Microcontroller deployment and toolchains

* **CMSIS-NN / Hello Edge** [V]: the original int8 Cortex-M KWS path; this repository's ancestor.
* **LiteRT for Microcontrollers** (formerly TensorFlow Lite Micro) [V]: core runtime ~16 KB on Cortex-M3; kernels for Cortex-M, Tensilica, ARC, RISC-V; vendor backends (Ethos-U, NXP eIQ, ST X-CUBE-AI). The `micro_speech` example is the canonical tiny-KWS deployment.
* **ESP-DL + ESP-PPQ** (our target path) [V]: ONNX-aligned operator set, int8 / int16 / mixed w8a16 symmetric quantization, PTQ from PyTorch/ONNX. **Verified from the operator support table**: ESP32-S3 (PIE V1 instructions) supports Conv (1-D/2-D; groups only 1 or in_channels, i.e. depthwise), Gemm, MatMul, Add, Mul, Pow, AveragePool/GlobalAveragePool, ReduceMean, Relu, Clip, LeakyRelu, PRelu, HardSwish, HardSigmoid, Sigmoid, Tanh, Elu, Swish, Exp, Log, Sqrt, Softmax/LogSoftmax (**float32 output**), LayerNormalization, RMSNormalization, LSTM, GRU, Transpose, Reshape, Concat, Pad, Slice, Split, Resize. **Absent: GELU/Erf and any scan/cumsum operator.** Conv/pool use NHWC/NWC layouts for SIMD.
  **Design consequences we took from this:** no GELU (rules out KWT/KW-MLP blocks as-is); mid-network Softmax breaks the int8 chain (attention is penalised); the selective scan needs a custom kernel; BatchNorm must be folded; SubSpectralNorm becomes a per-sub-band Mul + Add.
* **Hardware-aware architecture search for MCUs**: μNAS (EuroMLSys 2021) [V] searches under RAM / flash / latency constraints; MicroNets (MLSys 2021) [S] ties latency to operation count on MCUs; data-aware differentiable NAS for tiny KWS (July 2025) [V] searches over *data* configuration (e.g. feature size) jointly with architecture; hardware-aware training for efficient KWS (tinyML 2021) [V].
* **Pruning/quantization studies on low-power edge MCUs for KWS** (2022) [S].

## 7. State-space models on constrained hardware

This is the area closest to our model, and it is **young but no longer empty**:

* **MambaLite-Micro** (Sept 2025) [S]: notes that Mamba's reference implementation depends on custom GPU kernels and **has no ONNX export path**, which blocks standard embedded toolchains; benchmarks memory-optimised Mamba inference on MCUs.
* **FEMBA on the Edge** (2026) [S]: a bidirectional-Mamba EEG foundation model quantized and deployed on the **GAP9 RISC-V MCU**; input/output projections and gate generation in INT8, **the recurrent selective-scan path kept in float32** because it is quantization-sensitive.
* **BabyMamba-HAR** (2026) [S]: lightweight selective SSMs for human-activity recognition on constrained devices.
* **SSM quantization**: Quamba2 (ICML 2025) [S] — post-training quantization for selective SSMs, W8A8/W4A8/W4A16; MambaQuant (2501.13484) [S] — variance-aligned rotation; Quamba-SE (2026) [S] — soft-edge activation quantizer for SSMs.

**What this means for us.** "First Mamba on an MCU" is **not available as a claim**. "Streaming selective-SSM KWS deployed and measured on an ESP32-S3" appears unclaimed, but it is an engineering-first contribution. The repeated observation that *the recurrent path resists quantization* is, however, an opening: nobody has characterised **why**, or exploited the contractivity of `exp(ΔA) < 1` to bound error accumulation (see §11).

## 8. Surveys, benchmarks, competitions

* **Advances in Small-Footprint Keyword Spotting** (June 2025; journal version in Neurocomputing) [V]: ~250 papers, 2010–2025, organised into model architecture, learning techniques, compression, attention, feature optimisation, NAS, hybrids; reports CNNs 43.9 % and DNNs 36.6 % of surveyed architectures, int8 quantization shrinking models by up to 69 % at maintained accuracy. Lists open directions: hardware–software co-design, multilingual/code-switched KWS, federated/privacy-preserving training, user-customisable keywords, neuromorphic inference. **This is the single best entry point for a related-work section.**
* **MLPerf Tiny** [V]: the benchmark defining the MCU KWS task (DS-CNN on V2-12, 90 % accuracy target, latency + energy). **v1.3 (Sept 2025)** added the streaming wake-word task; **v1.4 (July 2026)** had nine submitters, with dedicated accelerators appearing in MCU-class parts (ASYGN ColibriNPU 22.2 µJ/inference on Visual Wake Words; Syntiant NDP120 at 3.3 % duty cycle on streaming wake word; STMicroelectronics 23.3 % lower power on image classification).
* **Deep spoken KWS: an overview** (2021) [S]; **Auto-KWS challenge** (2021) [S]; DCASE-style low-complexity tasks are the analogous benchmark culture in acoustic scene classification [M].

## 10. How our contributions sit against this literature

| Component of ours | Closest prior work | Honest status |
|---|---|---|
| Broadcast-residual conv backbone | BC-ResNet (2021) | reused as published |
| Bidirectional selective SSM over time | Mamba (2023); Keyword Mamba (2025) | reused; our model is ~17× smaller |
| Conv front-end + SSM back-end | common pattern across audio/vision; MASV for speaker verification [S] | **engineering combination, not a contribution** |
| Ensemble knowledge distillation into a tiny KWS student | ensemble KD + curriculum learning for noise-robust SF-KWS (IEEE Access 2023) | **prior art exists**; our teacher composition finding (a *diverse* 3-model teacher beats a stronger but homogeneous 4-model one, by validation) is a small empirical result |
| **NASS**: supervised speech-presence gate scaling Δ in a selective SSM, with free frame-level SNR targets | VAD-gated KWS cascades [M]; Skip RNN (learned skipping, unsupervised) | **not found in searches**; the novel element is *supervising the gate from the mixing SNR we create ourselves* and placing it inside the state update |
| **BC-SSM**: SSM replacing the temporal conv inside the broadcast path | BC-ResNet (conv), Keyword Mamba (SSM over the whole sequence) | **not found**, but it produced **no clean-accuracy gain** in a parameter-matched comparison — reported as a negative result |
| Protocol-equivalence measurement across the three 12-class test-set constructions | not found stated anywhere | small but useful methodological contribution |
| Streaming selective-SSM KWS on an ESP32-S3 | MambaLite-Micro, FEMBA/GAP9, BabyMamba-HAR (other tasks/MCUs) | **not claimable as "first Mamba on MCU"**; "first for KWS on this class of device" pending verification |

## 11. Gaps that look genuinely open

Derived from the above, not from a systematic gap analysis:

1. **Why the recurrent path resists quantization.** Two independent MCU deployments keep the selective scan in float [S] without analysing it. Because `exp(ΔA) < 1`, the recurrence is contractive and quantization error from step *t−k* decays geometrically, which should permit a steady-state error bound and a principled per-channel bit allocation. Untouched as far as we can see.
2. **Recurrent state across power failures.** The intermittent-computing literature (e.g. inference on energy-harvesting devices, ASPLOS-class work [M]) assumes stateless inference; a model whose entire memory is a decaying state suggests *decay-aware* checkpointing.
3. **Streaming execution planning in MCU toolchains.** Every toolchain in §6 is whole-window; per-layer ring buffers and cross-frame memory planning are hand-written. Our own numbers (189 KB whole-window vs ~24 KB streaming peak for the same model) show the size of the prize.
4. **Hard real-time guarantees.** Embedded ML papers report mean latency; always-on audio has a 10 ms deadline per frame. Anytime inference with WCET-style analysis bridges two communities that rarely cite each other.
5. **Distillation as an attack/robustness surface for tiny models.** §4 shows distillation changes robustness in ways nobody isolates; the security version (what a manipulated teacher transfers into a quantized student) appears unexamined.
6. **Noise-robustness evaluation hygiene.** Most KWS papers report clean accuracy only; those reporting noise mostly mix the *same* noise recordings used in training (ours included — see `EXPERIMENTS.md` E19). An unseen-noise protocol (MUSAN/DEMAND) ought to be standard.
